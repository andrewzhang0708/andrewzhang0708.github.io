All the (Virtual) World's a Stage

Enabling Audience Participation in VR Theater

Authors: Andrew Zhang, Arthur Caetano, Tobias Höllerer
Affiliation: Four Eyes Lab, University of California, Santa Barbara

Overview

What happens when an audience member becomes a character in a theatrical performance?

Virtual Reality opens new possibilities for audiences to participate in dramatic worlds, not merely as observers, but as characters who can interact with performers, influence their surroundings, and contribute to the unfolding experience.

However, inviting an audience member into a story is not as simple as giving them an avatar or interactive controls. How does a production establish their identity as a character? How does it communicate their role, responsibilities, and opportunities for participation?

In this ongoing research, we investigate how participatory VR theater productions establish and support audience members' participation as characters within a dramatic world.

Research Approach

Our research begins at the intersection of VR design theory and theater theory, bringing together two perspectives on how audiences experience and participate in mediated performances.

VR research provides concepts such as immersion, presence, embodiment, and agency, while theater theory offers perspectives on spectatorship, character, dramaturgy, and the relationships between performers and audiences. We explore where these theoretical perspectives converge, complement each other, or reveal different understandings of participation.

Building on this interdisciplinary foundation, we conduct a review of existing VR theater productions and relevant literature to investigate how different productions establish character identity, communicate dramatic relationships, structure interactions, and balance authored narratives with audience agency.

By connecting theoretical concepts with observable production strategies, we aim to develop a design framework that links intended audience experiences to actionable design choices in participatory VR theater.

Research Contribution

Rather than prescribing a single model of participation, this work seeks to identify a design space for participatory VR theater, helping researchers and creators reason about how different interaction and dramaturgical choices shape the audience's role within a performance.

By bridging perspectives from VR and theater, we hope to provide a shared conceptual foundation for understanding audience participation, translating insights from both disciplines into practical guidance for designing interactive theatrical experiences.

The framework also informs my ongoing creative research project, Becoming Prospero, which explores character embodiment and interpretation within an authored Shakespearean narrative.

Status: Ongoing research. The proposed framework and manuscript are under development.
