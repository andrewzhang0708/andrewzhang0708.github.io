# Becoming Prospero

*Exploring Embodiment, Audience Agency, and Interpretation in Participatory VR Theater*

**Research Project · Work in Progress**

**Unity · Meta Quest 3 · Hand Tracking · Interactive Narrative · VR Theater**

## Overview

*Becoming Prospero* is an ongoing research and creative project that reimagines Shakespeare's *The Tempest* as a first-person, participatory virtual reality experience.

Traditionally, audiences observe Prospero's journey from vengeance toward forgiveness through an actor's performance. But what happens when the audience becomes Prospero?

In this single-player, asynchronous VR experience, participants inhabit Prospero's perspective, speak lines from Shakespeare's original text, and perform magic through hand-tracked gestures. Other characters, including Ariel, are represented by prerecorded performances.

Rather than transforming *The Tempest* into a branching narrative, *Becoming Prospero* preserves its authored dramatic arc while introducing opportunities for embodied participation.

## Research Questions

**How might embodying a dramatic character change an audience member's interpretation of the story, its characters, and their relationships?**

The project explores three closely related questions:

- **Embodiment and Interpretation:** How might experiencing a character's actions from a first-person perspective influence the audience's understanding of their motivations and transformation?
- **Agency within Authored Narratives:** How can interactive experiences create meaningful opportunities for participation when the narrative outcome remains predetermined?
- **Interaction as Dramatic Expression:** How can gestures, dialogue, and spatial relationships communicate theatrical themes such as power, responsibility, and forgiveness?

## Design Approach

The relationship between Prospero and Ariel serves as a central dramatic focus. Prospero's authority over Ariel, his exercise of magical power, and his eventual decision to embrace forgiveness provide opportunities to explore how interaction design can communicate relationships and themes through action.

By inviting participants to speak Prospero's words and physically perform his actions, the project investigates the possibilities of experiencing Shakespearean language not only as spectators, but as participants within the dramatic world.

The experience does not require participants to be trained actors. Instead, it explores how VR might make aspects of theatrical performance and interpretation accessible through carefully designed interactions.

## Research Motivation

My interest in this project grew from my experiences performing and directing Shakespeare, including portraying Ariel in a stage production of *The Tempest*. Having experienced the play from the perspective of a performer, I became curious about what might change when the audience is invited to occupy the position of a character.

*Becoming Prospero* brings together theatrical practice, interaction design, and research into audience experience. It explores how computational media might offer new forms of dramatic participation without replacing the expressive qualities of theater.

**Project Status:** In development. An early interactive VR prototype has been created, and the broader research questions and design approach are being refined.
